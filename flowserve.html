Working environment

Industrial valve manufacturing

Engineering, maintenance, EHS, and operations teams

Production work instructions

I authored and revised procedures for painting, rust prevention, shipping, sanding, and smear testing. An approved smear-test work instruction was implemented in production. The work involved turning shop-floor processes into clear, repeatable instructions.

Drawing interpretation and safety support

I interpreted industrial valve drawings to determine custom shipping requirements and supported evaluation of test-bay enclosures for pressure capacity and safety requirements. These tasks connected technical drawings with material handling and the production environment.

Air-quality data

I analyzed paint-booth emissions and air-quality data in Excel to track VOC usage and support environmental reporting. This strengthened my experience organizing operational data and communicating findings.

Equipment traceability

I documented more than 500 lifting devices and created standardized identification labels and safety materials to improve equipment tracking and inspection readiness.

Facility planning

I worked with maintenance, EHS, and operations teams on CAPEX proposals and vendor reviews for more than $60,000 in potential facility and equipment improvements. This amount represents proposed upgrades, rather than realized savings.

What I learned

Good engineering has to work on the floor.

Usable documentation, accurate records, and communication with operators and maintenance staff support the technical work. My internship showed me how engineering decisions connect to day-to-day production needs.
