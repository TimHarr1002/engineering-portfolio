01 / Mechanical design and fabrication

Electric boat propulsion

Designing and fabricating the drivetrain and motor mounting system for GW’s electric boat.

GW Electric Yacht ClubDecember 2025 – PresentSolidWorks · Onshape · Machining

My role

CAD and manufacturing team member

40+hours of machine-shop fabrication
ME1616Motenergy propulsion motor

The challenge

Develop a drivetrain and motor mounting system to transfer power from the electric motor to the propeller, integrating the propulsion and steering hardware into the boat for the ASNE Promoting Electric Propulsion competition.

Modeling the system

I modeled the ME1616 motor from technical drawings and physical measurements, then designed the drivetrain assembly, including its shaft, bearings, gears, and thrust components. I created 3D models, assemblies, and engineering drawings to support fabrication and integration.

Structural analysis

I evaluated the motor mount using finite element analysis for expected propulsion and impact loads, including structural analysis in Onshape. This connected the CAD geometry to the loads the mounting system needed to support.

Building and integrating

I led more than 40 hours of machine-shop fabrication, producing transom mounts, side arms, motor mount panels, brackets, connecting blocks, steering supports, and structural plates. I collaborated with the team to assemble, test, and troubleshoot the propulsion and steering systems under a compressed competition schedule.

Reverse engineering for printing

I reverse-engineered the boat’s front cowl and created a SolidWorks model intended for 3D printing.

Project evidence

Models and machined components.

What I learned

CAD is one part of a working assembly.

Component fit, fabrication, mounting interfaces, and system integration all matter when a design becomes hardware. This project gave me practice carrying a design into the shop and solving assembly problems with a team.
