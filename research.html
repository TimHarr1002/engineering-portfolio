Camera and IMU research

Working with stereo-camera and inertial-sensor recordings to evaluate visual inertial odometry.

The question

How consistently do two visual inertial odometry pipelines estimate motion from the same recording? VIO combines camera images and inertial measurements to estimate position and orientation.

My contribution

I configured a Linux environment using Python, C++, and connected GitHub repositories; collected and reviewed headset camera and motion-sensor recordings in MCAP format using Foxglove; and worked on the calibration and VIO workflow.

Comparing the pipelines

The same walking recording was processed through OpenVINS and sqrt-vins. The analysis compared pose counts, path lengths, processing time, and end-to-start distances. Both trajectories were translated so their initial position was at the origin for visualization.

Interpreting the results

The cross-estimator absolute trajectory error was approximately 115.5 mm, or 11.6 cm. sqrt-vins completed processing 34 seconds faster, while OpenVINS ended closer to the starting point.

Because there was no external ground-truth trajectory, this result measures agreement between estimators rather than absolute position accuracy. It comes from one trial, so it does not establish which pipeline is better across other conditions.

What I learned

A result needs a reference.

Sensor calibration and consistent recordings make comparisons useful. Equally important is understanding what the metrics can establish: close agreement between two estimates does not by itself prove accuracy.
